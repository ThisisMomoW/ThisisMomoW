# Mo Wang

I'm a master's student in biostatistics at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. My research focuses on causal inference with incomplete data, especially how to estimate treatment effects when exposure is missing for some study participants.

## Featured project

[**Causal inference with missing exposures**](https://github.com/ThisisMomoW/missing-exposure-simulation)

An R simulation study comparing methods for estimating average treatment effects with a partially observed binary exposure. The study examines different missingness mechanisms, nonlinear relationships, and how including the outcome in exposure imputation affects the estimates. The repository includes simulation code, replication results, and scripts to regenerate the figures.

## Current research

- **Causal inference with missing exposures.** My thesis compares ways to estimate the models used in treatment effect estimation and studies how those choices affect stability and confidence interval coverage in small samples.
- **Gene regulation.** I use random forests to predict gene expression from bulk DNase accessibility profiles and study whether distal regions improve prediction beyond regions near transcription start sites.
